# Class A Transistor Drive

A stereo plugin modeled after the [North Coast Synthesis MSK 011 Transistor Mixer](https://files.northcoastsynthesis.com/msk-011.pdf), a GPLv3 design by Matthew Skala. Each stereo side simulates a separate copy of one channel and the full mixer/amplifier network. This is a reduced transistor model, not a complete physical simulation or a hardware measurement. Trademarks belong to their respective owners. The template is MIT-licensed; its VST3 builds use GPLv3 bindings and are GPLv3. Consult all applicable licenses before redistribution.

## Circuit and controls

- The component values and topology come from the manual's schematic (p. 22), with circuit description on pp. 18–20. Channel 2 is the audio input: it is ground-normalled rather than one of the outer DC-offset channels. The only control, **Level**, models its 100 kΩ audio-taper pot (manual p. 7); the template approximates its taper by two linear segments. The other three input jacks are unpatched and their level knobs fully counterclockwise. Channel 1 is still normalized through 100 kΩ to +12 V, channel 4 through 100 kΩ to -12 V, and channel 3 to ground; each of the four input followers remains in the circuit.
- The AC output includes the 4.7 µF coupling capacitor and 330 kΩ shunt, with an assumed 100 kΩ external load. Input source resistance is assumed 1 kΩ. Rails are ideal ±12 V; power supply protection/decoupling are not simulated. A full-scale digital peak corresponds to 5 V, so a full-scale sine is 10 Vpp; the same scale is used on output. No hardware calibration or component tolerance variation is claimed.
- As directed by the offset-null procedure (p. 15), with all level controls off and no input, the 100 kΩ trim was numerically adjusted until the DC output was zero. Reduced-model SPICE gives 35,009.67484 Ω from +12 V to wiper and 64,990.32516 Ω from wiper to -12 V. AC output has zero DC. Pots are clamped to at least 1 Ω at the ends; zero-ohm transistor emitter resistance uses 0.001 Ω in Rust for numerical stability, versus zero in SPICE.

## Transistor-model limitations

The reduced Gummel–Poon model uses Fairchild/onsemi's [2N5088 model](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/2n5088-d.pdf): IS=5.911 fA, BF=1122, BR=1.271, VAF=62.37 V, IKF=14.92 mA, RB=10 Ω, RC=1.61 Ω, RE=0 Ω (RE is omitted in the vendor model, so uses the SPICE default). These eight values are identical in Rust and `reference/circuit.cir`. Both omit junction/transit charge storage (CJE, CJC, TF, TR and associated parameters) and leakage (ISE/NE/ISC/NC). The vendor model actually has nonzero ISE and charge storage: passing validation therefore establishes agreement with the **reduced** model, not the full vendor model or an actual unit.

At the reduced-model calibrated bias, the four followers conduct about 0.71 mA each; Q5 and Q6 conduct about 2.93 mA and 2.55 mA. For a follower VBE ≈ 0.6563 V at 27 °C, the vendor leakage equation predicts roughly 0.475 µA of extra base current versus 0.552 µA ideal base current. Approximate DC beta thus falls from 1288 to 692, so leakage is not negligible for base loading, even though collector bias shifts are modest.

A rough upper-bound Miller estimate for Q5 uses CJC=4.017 pF, Rsum ≈ 2.20 kΩ, and an unloaded voltage gain magnitude ≈ 9.86, giving about 43.6 pF and a 1.66 MHz pole. This is only an estimate: bias-dependent capacitances, gain, other devices, and transit charge matter. An independent ngspice comparison (`python3 reference/check_fidelity.py 1`, 27 °C, full level, same calibrated trim) finds at 20 kHz: reduced +2.1526 dB; adding only charge +2.1505 dB with ≈-1.67° phase change; adding only leakage +2.0573 dB; full vendor +2.0553 dB. The full vendor model moves the zero-input DC output from about -0.75 mV to -24.95 mV at this level. These small-signal results are not proof of equivalence under clipping or at other settings.

## Validation and use

Run `./validate.sh` for full SPICE comparison; reports and plots are in `validation/`. If switching between `QUICK=1` and full, remove the generated `validation/` directory before running: the template caches packs and references without tracking mode. The settings cover default, maximum, 0.05 rotation, and zero. Test using `cargo test` and `cd harness && uv run pytest -q`; build/install with `./install.sh` and rescan the DAW.

**CPU warning:** the final benchmark measured **240.7% of one core per stereo instance at 48 kHz**, so this build cannot be expected to run in real time on one core on the test machine. Measure on yours with `cargo run --release --example bench -p dsp 48000`. The initial loose-solver benchmark was 176.6%; the final model tightens the inter-device sweep criterion to remove spurious low-level harmonics. No validation tolerances were relaxed.

The full (not QUICK) validation completed with default, low (0.05), and high (1.0) all PASS; a later run with each section rendered from a fresh DC state also passed at level 0 (2026-11-17). The figures that follow are from the first run. Across those settings the worst errors were: frequency response 0.02896 dB; static gain 0.001948 dB; harmonics 0.1084 dB; intermodulation 0.04717 dB; burst waveform null -78.89 dB relative to reference. Attack/release tests detected no gain change in either simulation. Nine Rust tests and seven harness tests passed. Installed VST3 and CLAP bundles were verified on disk and passed code-signature verification.
